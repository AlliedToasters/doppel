The last two posts gave Maia-3 a description of the player, then a learned search through their earlier games. Both
helped, by a quarter to a third of a point. This post tries the bluntest idea of all: train a separate small model for
every player.

## A few hundred thousand numbers per person

Maia-3 has 79 million parameters and stays frozen. For each player we add a *low-rank adapter*: a small correction
to four of its layers, 196,608 numbers in all, about 390 KB. We train it on the player's 200 most recent earlier games
(strictly before the test game), keep their 5 latest games aside to decide when to stop, and then score the test game.

Training one player takes about 0.7 seconds on one GPU. On a 16-thread CPU it takes about 80 seconds.

As a control, we score every test game a second time through *someone else's* adapter, from the same rating band.

A player's own adapter gains about a point in every band up to 2000. Someone else's costs two to almost three.
The adapter is learning the person, not the rating band.

Above 2000 the own adapter gains nothing. That turned out to be a volume problem, not a missing signal.

## Speaking more quietly

Where does the adapter go wrong? When Maia-3's top two moves are nearly tied, the adapter helps a lot: that is where a
person's habits decide the move. It hurts in a different place: when Maia-3 has a moderate favourite, the adapter
sometimes overrules it, more often late in the game. It is pointing the right way but too loudly.

So we mixed it with Maia-3: Maia-3 plus a fraction *w* of the adapter's change. We chose *w* on the players' own
earlier games, not on the test set.

The best strength is **0.625**, in every band (0.5 to 0.625), and the same in each of five folds of players. Turn
it up past 1 and it gets worse fast.

## How big is that?

A point and a bit: 115 more correct moves per 10,000, about three times the learned lookup from the last post and more
than the whole spread between the best published models on this test. It costs a second of compute and 390 KB per
player, which is what a Doppel bot can actually ship.

Two caveats:

1. **Most of the gain is in the opening.** At moves 10–19 the full-strength adapter gains 2.6 to 4 points; after move 40 it adds
   little. Style in new middlegames is still the hard part.
2. **The players need a history.** Many of the lowest-rated players have fewer than 200 earlier games. A new player
   with no games gets plain Maia-3, for now.

## What's next

Each player now has an adapter: a point in a space of a few hundred thousand dimensions. The next post asks what that
space looks like. Do players fall into types, or is it a continuum? And which parts of Maia-3 carry the difference?
