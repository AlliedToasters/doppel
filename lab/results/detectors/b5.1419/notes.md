# b5.1419 notes

1. **Description.** Fires on the "contact zone" between the side to move's developed pieces and the enemy's pawn/king cover: our developed knight/bishop/queen (and our king next to our queen), empty squares that an enemy pawn or king guards and that sit next to our queen or are eyed by our bishop/queen, plus the opponent's advanced queen (and, in thinned-out positions, their rook/king) when it closes on our men.

2. **Evidence and what I tried.** Piece class is the biggest factor. Pawns, our rooks and the enemy's minor pieces almost never fire. Our N/B/K/Q fire on 21-34% of their tokens, the enemy queen on 36%, and empty squares on 2.4%, but empty squares still make up about 42% of all firings. For empty squares, firing needs enemy pawn/king guard and then falls off steeply with king distance to our own queen (guarded and adjacent to our queen: 48%; distance 2: 15%; distance 3: 6%). For pieces, firing goes up with the number of attacked squares that the enemy pawns/king guard ("contact"), and is suppressed on the home rank, under attack, in check, and in the opening or with few pieces left. The enemy queen fires most when it is advanced and hits our men. Their rook/king fire in endgames near our king. Move features (Maia top-5 and Stockfish PV from/to squares, captures) were essentially uninformative. A GBM on many board features reaches only about 0.70-0.76 within-class AUC for our pieces, so a large share of the variance is not captured by simple board features. Spearman conflicts with AUROC because of the GELU shape: pawns sit near 0 while near-threshold empty squares sit near -0.17. So the score adds 0.4 times a per-class "silent median" to a firing-probability term.

3. **Final dev metrics** (10 scoring runs): Spearman 0.237 (spearman_pos 0.283), AUROC 0.869, F1 0.420 at threshold 0.11. The baseline was F1 0.199 (`our_N & legal_from`). An always-on detector gets F1 ≈ 0.072.

4. **Caveats.** Within developed own knights/bishops/king the detector is barely better than chance (per-class AUROC 0.63-0.71). Firing there is about 40% and largely unexplained. The empty-square and their-queen parts are better (AUROC 0.82 / 0.77). The neuron looks polysemantic or at least broad: several loosely related triggers (own active pieces, queen-neighbourhood guarded squares, enemy queen activity, endgame rook/king approach). You can tell a common "front-line contact" story, but it is a stretch. Raising λ trades AUROC for Spearman (λ=2 gives Spearman about 0.41 and AUROC about 0.79). Confidence: low-medium.

Scripts: explore*.py, feat.py, tree.py, etree.py, pc.py, heat.py, evalcls.py, dump.py (dev only).
